## 1. League Overview

Welcome! The HCR Sim Racing League (herein after referred to as “HCR League” or “The League”) is an iRacing focused endurance and sprint format sim racing league inspired heavily on the real-life IMSA
WeatherTech SportsCar Championship and its associated leagues and competitions. The primary goal of the
HCR League is to create a virtual platform for fun and competitive online racing for everyone.
The HCR League will follow the full IMSA WeatherTech SportsCar Championship schedule as closely as possible. Every HCR League race will be based on a real IMSA event and will be run at half the length of the official real-life races.
The league will focus on:
- Multi-class IMSA-style racing
- Realistic race formats
- Team-based endurance racing
- Clean driving
- Fair competition
- Strategy, pit stops, and driver changes
- Professional race control and stewarding

## 2. Official Platform

HCR League will run exclusively on iRacing. All official league races, practices, qualifying sessions, and events will take place on the iRacing service.
Participants are responsible for having:
- An active iRacing account
- The required cars
- The required tracks
- A stable internet connection
- Proper sim racing equipment (Steering wheel with pedals)
- Basic understanding of iRacing rules and race procedures
- Adhering to all applicable rules and regulations of the iRacing Terms of Service and the HCR League at all
times
HCR League rules will apply in addition to iRacing’s rules and code of conduct.

## 3. League Classes

HCR League will use three classes: GTP, LMP2, and GTD. There will be no GTD PRO class in HCR League. All
GT cars will race together in the GTD class.

### 3.1 GTP Class

The GTP class is the top prototype class in HCR League. These cars are the fastest cars on track and are expected to be driven by experienced and aware drivers.
- Fastest overall class
- Responsible for clean traffic management
- Must be patient when overtaking LMP2 and GTD cars
- Must not force slower cars off their racing line
- Must avoid unnecessary risks in traffic
- Higher race awareness is expected

### 3.2 LMP2 Class

The LMP2 class is the second prototype class. LMP2 cars are faster than GTD cars but slower than GTP cars.
- Manage traffic from both faster GTP cars and slower GTD cars
- Stay predictable when being overtaken by GTP cars
- Make clean and fair passes on GTD cars
- Respect class battles happening around them
- Avoid aggressive moves in traffic

### 3.3 GTD Class

The GTD class is the main GT category in HCR League. Since there is no GTD PRO class, all GT drivers will compete together in GTD.
- Focus on clean racing and consistency
- Hold a predictable racing line when being lapped/passed by faster traffic
- Avoid sudden moves in traffic
- Respect prototype traffic
- Race fairly within class battles
- Do not block faster-class cars intentionally and yield to blue flag rules

## 4. Driver Eligibility

To compete in HCR League, drivers must meet the following requirements:
1. Drivers must join the official HCR League Discord.
2. Drivers must read and agree to the full rulebook.
3. Drivers must register using their real name, iRacing profile, or approved racing name.
4. Drivers must have an active iRacing account.
5. Drivers must own the required car and track for each race they enter.
6. Drivers must have a stable internet connection.
7. Drivers must race respectfully and safely.
8. Drivers must follow all race control and stewarding instructions and decisions.
HCR League staff may deny entry, suspend, or otherwise remove a driver if they believe the driver is unsafe, disrespectful, or harmful to their participating class or the league as a whole.

## 5. Team Registration

Each entry must register before competing in an HCR League event. Registration must include:
- Full Team Name
- Driver name(s) or driver lineup
- Car number
- Class
- Car choice
- Livery, if applicable
- Primary team contact
- Discord name(s)
- iRacing name(s)
Each car entry is considered its own team entry for scoring purposes, even if one organization runs multiple cars.

## 6. Season-Long Car Selection

Drivers and teams must choose one car to run for the full HCR League season. Once a team registers its car for the season, that car must be used for every race the team enters.

### 6.1 Car Selection Rules

- Each team must declare its car before the first race of the season.
- Only cars participating in the current IMSA season are eligible to be selected
- Teams may not switch cars during the season.
- The selected car must remain in the same class for the full season.
- A team may not switch from one GTD car to another GTD car mid-season.
- A team may not switch from LMP2 to GTP, GTD to LMP2, or any other class change during the season.
- If a team changes cars without approval, they may lose points from any race where the unapproved car was
used..

### 6.2 Exceptions

HCR League staff may approve a car change in special circumstances, such as:
- iRacing removes a car license.
- iRacing significantly updates a car in a way that creates a major performance issue.
- A car becomes unavailable for league use.
- A major iRacing update creates an unfair or unusable car issue.
- League staff makes a season-wide class or car adjustment.
Car changes for performance reasons will not normally be approved.

## 7. Car Numbers

Each car must select a number during registration.

### 7.1 Number Rules

- Numbers must be between 0 and 999.
- No two cars may use the same number, no matter what class they are in.
- Car numbers are assigned on a first-come, first-served basis unless staff grants priority to a returning team.
- Returning teams may receive priority for their previous number.
- Offensive or inappropriate number designs are not allowed.
- League staff may reject a number if it causes confusion.
Example: If a GTP car uses number 10, no LMP2 or GTD car may also use number 10.

## 8. Liveries

Custom liveries are allowed and encouraged.

### 8.1 Livery Requirements

All liveries must:
- Be appropriate and respectful
- Include the correct car number
- Match the registered car
- Not include offensive language or imagery
- Not copy another team’s livery too closely
- Include HCR League required decals if provided
- Be submitted before the livery deadline

### 8.2 Livery Approval

League staff may exercise their discretion on liveries at any time and may require livery changes without prior notice. Teams may be asked to make changes before being approved to participate using a given livery.

## 9. Event Format

HCR League events are designed to simulate real IMSA-style race weekends while keeping the format simple for iRacing.
Each race event will include:
1. Practice (1 session)
2. Qualifying
3. Race

### 9.1 Practice

Practice sessions allow drivers to learn the track, test setups, check fuel strategy, prepare for traffic, and get ready for race conditions. Drivers are expected to follow clean driving standards during practice. Intentional wrecking, blocking, reckless driving, or distracting behavior may result in penalties or further disciplinary action.

### 9.2 Qualifying

Qualifying determines the starting order for the race. Qualifying may be handled in one of the following formats:
- Open qualifying
- Private qualifying
- Lone qualifying
- Class-based qualifying
- Team qualifying
League staff will announce the qualifying format before each event.

### 9.3 Race

The race will begin after qualifying. Drivers are responsible for being present, prepared, and in the iRacing server before the race starts. All HCR League races will be run at exactly half the length of the official real-life
IMSA race that the league event is based on.

## 10. Race Starts

All HCR League races will use rolling starts. Standing starts will not be used.

### 10.1 Rolling Start Procedure

1. Drivers must follow the iRacing pace car, start procedure, and race control instructions.
2. Drivers must stay in their assigned starting position.
3. Drivers must maintain a safe and reasonable gap.
4. No excessive weaving is allowed.
5. No brake-checking is allowed.
6. No laying back to gain an advantage is allowed. All Drivers, including the leader, must maintain a consistent pace until the race begins.
7. The race begins when iRacing displays the green flag or when race control announces green.
8. Passing before the green flag is not allowed unless another car clearly has a problem.
9. Slower classes are expected to maintain a roughly 15 second gap to the faster class(es) starting in front of them until the race begins.

### 10.2 Jump Starts

A driver may be penalized for:
- Passing before green
- Accelerating too early
- Laying back to gain an advantage
- Causing contact before the green flag
- Ignoring iRacing or race control start procedures

Every HCR Endurance Cup race requires at least one driver change. This means:

## 13. Sprint Races

All HCR League races that are not part of the real-life IMSA Michelin Endurance Cup will be classified as sprint races.
Sprint races include:
- Long Beach
- Laguna Seca
- Detroit Belle Isle
- Canadian Tire Motorsport Park
- VIR
- Indianapolis

### 13.1 Sprint Race Driver Rules

A driver change is not required during sprint races. This means:
- Sprint races may be completed by one driver.
- Teams may still use multiple drivers if league staff allows it for that event.
- No team will be penalized for completing a sprint race without a driver change.

### 13.2 Sprint Race Pit Stop Requirement

Every car must complete at least one pit stop during each sprint race. A valid pit stop means:
- The car must enter the pit lane.
- The car must come to a complete stop in its assigned pit box.
- The car must receive service while in the pit lane (tires and fuel).
- The stop must happen during the official race session prior to the start of the final lap.
- A drive-through penalty does not count as the required pit stop.
- A stop-and-go penalty does not count as the required pit stop unless league staff specifically announces
otherwise.

### 13.3 Missing Required Pit Stop

If a car does not complete the required pit stop during a sprint race, the team will receive:
- First Offense - 45 second time penalty and your team will start the next race on the last place on the
grid.
- Second Offense - Race Disqualification, and a one race suspension served for the next race event.
- Third Offense - Season Disqualification and potential expulsion from the HCR League.

## 14. Multi-Class Racing Rules

### 14.1 Responsibility of Faster Cars

Faster cars are responsible for passing safely. GTP and LMP2 drivers must understand that GTD cars are often already driving near their limit. Faster cars should not expect GTD cars to move off the racing line.
- Plan overtakes early
- Avoid aggressive maneuvers
- Be patient in corners
- Complete passes safely
- Leave enough room
- Respect class battles
- Avoid forcing slower cars off-track

### 14.2 Responsibility of Slower Cars

Slower cars must be predictable. GTD cars are not required to jump out of the way, but they must avoid sudden movements. GTD cars are expected to maintain the racing line when racing near faster classes.
- Hold their normal racing line
- Avoid sudden blocking moves
- Avoid changing lines under braking
- Use your mirrors and relative timing black box
- Let faster cars pass safely when reasonable

### 14.3 Class Battles

Cars fighting for position in their own class have the right to race. Faster-class cars must be careful when passing cars that are actively battling. A faster car ruining a slower-class battle through impatient driving may receive a penalty.

### 14.4 Flashing Lights

Flashing headlights may be used only as a warning or communication tool. Flashing lights does not give a faster car the automatic right to pass. Excessive flashing may be considered unsportsmanlike conduct and will be penalized as deemed appropriate.

## 15. General Driving Standards

### 15.1 Clean Racing Expectations

All drivers are expected to race cleanly and respectfully.
- Leave racing room when required
- Avoid intentional contact
- Avoid reckless moves
- Respect blue flags
- Respect yellow flags
- Rejoin safely after leaving the track
- Avoid blocking unfairly
- Drive with awareness
- Respect all classes on track

### 15.2 Racing Room

If two cars are significantly alongside each other entering a corner, both drivers must leave racing room. A car is generally considered alongside if its front axle is at least near the other car’s rear axle before the braking zone or turn-in point.

### 15.3 Blocking

Drivers may make one defensive move on a straight. Multiple moves in reaction to the car behind are not allowed. Blocking includes:
- Moving late in reaction
- Weaving on a straight
- Forcing a car off track
- Changing line under braking
- Preventing a faster car from fairly attempting a pass

### 15.4 Divebombs

A divebomb is a late, unrealistic, or uncontrolled passing attempt that causes contact or forces another car off track. Divebombs may result in penalties even if the attacking car is significantly alongside or completes a pass.

### 15.5 Unsafe Rejoin

Drivers who leave the track must rejoin safely. Unsafe rejoin examples include but are not limited to:
- Rejoining directly into traffic
- Spinning back onto the racing line
- Blocking cars while recovering
- Rejoining without checking oncoming traffic
- Cutting corners/intentionally leaving the racing surface

## 16. Contact and Incident Rules

### 16.1 Racing Incidents

A racing incident may be declared when:
- Both/all drivers contributed
- No driver was clearly at fault
- The incident was unavoidable
- Other relevant factors as determined by the steward team

### 16.2 Avoidable Contact

Avoidable contact happens when a driver causes contact that could have reasonably been prevented. Examples may include:
- Rear-ending/punting another car
- Turning into a car already alongside
- Missing a braking zone
- Forcing another car off track
- Unsafely rejoining the racing surface

### 16.3 Intentional Contact/Wrecking

Intentional wrecking, brake-checking, retaliation, or otherwise using the car as a weapon is strictly prohibited.
Intentional contact may result in:
- Race disqualification
- Time or Grid Penalties
- Points Deductions
- Race Suspension(s)
- Season Ban
- Permanent expulsion from all HCR competitions.

## 17. Track Limits

### 17.1 Standard Track Limit Rule

Drivers must follow iRacing’s track limits unless the HCR League announces a specific rule for an event. If iRacing issues an off-track or slowdown penalty, the driver is responsible for serving or clearing that penalty correctly unless otherwise instructed by Race Control.

### 17.2 Gaining an Advantage

If a driver leaves the racing surface and gains time or position, they must give the advantage back safely. Failure to give back an advantage may result in a penalty.

### 17.3 Repeated Abuse

Repeated track limit violations may result in:
- Warning
- Time penalty
- Drive-through or stop-and-go penalty
- Post-race or finishing position penalty
- Disqualification

## 18. Pit Stops

### 18.1 Pit Entry

Drivers must enter the pit lane safely. Drivers may not:
- Cut across the track dangerously
- Brake suddenly in front of traffic
- Observe the iRacing pit entry line and/or cone
- Enter pit lane from an unsafe angle
- Cross pit entry lines if iRacing or event rules prohibit it

### 18.2 Pit Exit

Drivers exiting pit lane must stay within the pit exit line until it ends as indicated by the blue cone provided by iRacing. Drivers re-entering the track must yield to all oncoming traffic and rejoin the racing surface safely.
Drivers on track should be aware of cars exiting the pit lane but are not required to give up the racing line unless needed to avoid contact.

### 18.3 Pit Speed

Drivers must obey the iRacing pit lane speed limit. Speeding in pit lane may result in:
- iRacing penalty
- Drive-through
- Stop-and-go
- Time penalty
- Other penalties deemed appropriate by the steward team and race control.

### 18.4 Pit Stall Safety

Drivers must avoid contact in pit lane. Unsafe pit entry, unsafe pit exit, or hitting another car in pit lane may result in a penalty.

## 19. Driver Swaps

### 19.1 Required Driver Changes

All HCR Endurance Cup races require at least one driver change. Sprint races do not require a driver change.

### 19.2 Driver Swap Requirements

League staff will announce the full driver swap rules before each Endurance Cup race, including:
- Minimum number of drivers
- Maximum number of drivers
- Minimum drive time, if used
- Maximum drive time, if used
- Required number of driver changes
- Any iRacing-specific driver swap rules

### 19.3 Missing Required Driver Change

If a team fails to complete the required driver change in an Endurance Cup race, the team may receive:
- Time penalty
- Points penalty
- Class disqualification
- Overall disqualification

### 19.4 Connection Issues During Driver Swaps

If a driver disconnects during a swap, the team must follow iRacing’s system and procedures. HCR League staff may review the situation, but lost laps or positions are not guaranteed to be restored.

## 20. Fuel, Tires, and Setups

### 20.1 Fuel

Fuel strategy is part of HCR League racing. Teams and drivers are responsible for calculating their own fuel.
The league may choose to use:
- Normal fuel usage
- Increased fuel usage
- Limited fuel tank size
- Required pit stops

### 20.2 Tires

Tire rules may vary by event. Possible tire rules include:
- Unlimited tire sets
- Limited tire sets
- Required tire change
- Optional tire change
- Class-specific tire rules

### 20.3 Setups

The league may use fixed setups, open setups, or limited setup changes. League staff will announce setup rules before each event.

## 21. Balance of Performance

### 21.1 BoP Adjustments

Balance of Performance, also known as BoP, may be used to keep classes and cars competitive. HCR League staff may adjust:
- Weight
- Power
- Fuel capacity
- Tire rules
- Class restrictions
- Car availability

### 21.2 BoP Suggestions

Drivers are encouraged to voice their feedback or concerns regarding BoP through the protest form on the
league website (hcrleague.com/portal). BoP discussion or input outside of this channel will not be considered.
All BoP decisions are final.

## 22. Cautions, Safety Cars, and Full Course Yellows

### 22.1 Local Yellow

A local yellow means there is an incident in one area of the track. Drivers must:
- Use caution
- Avoid passing near the incident
- Be prepared to slow down
- Avoid causing another incident

### 22.2 Safety Car Procedure

When a safety car is called, drivers must slow safely, maintain position, pace, and follow iRacing and race control instructions. Restarts will be announced by race control or handled by iRacing.

### 22.3 Restart Behavior

On restarts, drivers must follow all standard green flag procedures including but not limited to:
- Maintaining position
- Avoid brake-checking
- Avoiding jumping the restart
- Leaving appropriate racing room into Turn 1
- Following race control and iRacing procedures

## 23. Red Flags

### 23.1 Red Flag Conditions

A red flag may be used if the race cannot continue. Reasons may include a major server issue, mass disconnect, severe race control issue, or iRacing service issue. During a red flag, drivers must follow race control instructions. The race may be restarted, resumed, shortened, or declared official depending on the situation.

## 24. Blue Flags

### 24.1 For Slower Cars

Blue flags are informational. When shown a blue flag, slower cars should:
- Stay predictable
- Hold your line
- Do not brake suddenly
- Do not make sudden moves
- Allow the faster car to pass when safe

### 24.2 For Faster Cars

When approaching slower traffic, faster cars should:
- Be patient
- Make the pass safely
- Do not expect the slower car to accommodate you
- Avoid aggressive moves in dangerous areas

## 25. Communication Rules

### 25.1 Required Discord Channels

All official communication will happen through the HCR League Discord (Link Here). All drivers are required to join and regularly read announcements and news. Recommended channels include:
- #league-rulebook
- #season-info
- #league-general-chat

### 25.2 Voice Chat

Drivers may be required to join a driver briefing channel prior to the race event. There will be an optional Discord
League Chat Room available to all participating drivers. During official sessions, all drivers shall have the iRacing voice chat feature enabled. Voice chats should stay focused and relevant. Arguing, yelling, or distracting others during a race will result in penalties.

### 25.3 Race Control Communication

Drivers should not argue with race control during the race. If a driver has an issue, they should submit a protest after the race through the protest form on the league website (hcrleague.com/portal).

Drivers are expected to:
- Be respectful

The following behavior is not allowed:
- Harassment
- Hate speech
- Threats
- Intentional wrecking
- Cheating
- Exploiting game bugs
- Impersonating another driver
- Publicly attacking race control
- Sharing private staff discussions

- Any other conduct deemed to be detrimental to the league as determined by the HCR Administration

Depending on severity, conduct issues may result in:
- Warnings

- Driver name
- Team name

- Class
- Lap number

- Cars involved
- Replay clip or timestamp
- Short explanation

### 27.3 Protest Review

Stewards may decide:
- No further action
- Warning
- Time penalty
- Position penalty
- Points penalty
- Grid penalty for next race
- Race Disqualification

- Season Disqualification

### 27.4 False or Excessive Protests

Drivers who repeatedly submit poor, dishonest, or unnecessary protests may lose protest privileges.

## 28. Stewarding System

### 28.1 Steward Review Factors

Stewards will consider:
- Who caused the incident
- Whether the incident was avoidable
- How much damage was caused
- Whether positions were gained
- Whether time was lost
- Whether the driver waited or gave back position
- Previous driving history
- Race conditions
- Multi-class context

### 28.2 Steward Decisions

Steward decisions are final unless an appeal is allowed.

### 37.1 Broadcast Expectations

### 37.3 Clips and Highlights

Drivers are encouraged to submit clips for social media. Clips should not be used to harass or embarrass other drivers.

## 38. Cheating and Exploits

### 38.1 Cheating Policy

Cheating is not allowed. Cheating includes using illegal software, exploiting game bugs, manipulating connections, bypassing car restrictions, using unapproved car files, faking driver swaps, or sharing accounts without approval. Cheating will result in immediate expulsion from the HCR Racing league.

## 39. Connection and Technical Issues

### 39.1 Blinking or Lagging

If a driver is ‘blinking’ (lagging/disconnecting), race control may ask them to pit, disconnect, or retire.

### 39.2 Disconnects

If a driver disconnects, the race will usually continue. League staff are not required to restore laps or positions lost due to disconnects.

### 39.3 Server Failure

If the server fails, staff may restart the race, resume from previous results, shorten the race, declare official results, or reschedule the event.

## 40. Weather and Time of Day

### 40.1 Weather Settings

Events may use static weather, dynamic weather, realistic weather, or random weather depending on iRacing settings and league preference.

### 40.2 Rain Rules

If rain is used, drivers must adjust their driving style safely. Wet-weather incidents will still be reviewed under normal driving standards.

### 40.3 Night Racing

For night events, drivers must use headlights when required. Flashing headlights excessively is not allowed.

## 41. Official Results

### 41.1 Provisional Results

Initial results after the race are provisional.

### 41.2 Final Results

Results become final after protests are reviewed, penalties are applied, appeals are completed, and staff posts official results.

## 42. Rule Changes

### 42.1 Rule Updates

Rules contained herein may be changed at any time. Some examples that may trigger a rule change or update include: iRacing service changes, balance issues, safety concerns, schedule changes, or stewarding improvements.

### 42.2 Rule Update Notice

Staff will announce rule changes at least 48 hours before they take effect unless the change is urgent.

## 43. Staff Authority/Staff Participation

### 43.1 Final Authority

HCR League staff have final authority over entry approval, driver discipline, race control, schedule changes, penalties, rule interpretation, and championship format. Staff decisions will be fair, consistent, and focused on maintaining our core values as a league.

### 43.2 Participating Administrators/Staff

HCR League staff and administrators also love to race! Occasionally, HCR staff members may be racing in splits and are eligible to receive championship points. They are expected to hold themselves to an ever higher standard of sportsmanship and conduct.
Any supplementary discipline or protests regarding an in-race incident involving a staff member will be reviewed by another staff member who is not involved.
Staff members administering race control are strictly prohibited from participating in races in which they are conducting race control and supplementary discipline. This is important to maintain a fun and fair environment for everyone participating, including other HCR staff members.

## 44. Code of Conduct

### 44.1 Core Expectations

The HCR League exists to create competitive but fun racing. All drivers must remember to race hard but fair, respect other drivers, respect race control and league staff members, own their mistakes, keep the league fun, avoid ruining someone else’s race through careless driving, and treat the league like a real racing community.
